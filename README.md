# Chroniton Labs contributor agreements

Before your first contribution to any Chroniton Labs project is merged, you sign a contributor agreement. You sign once, and it covers every Chroniton Labs project.

| Agreement | Who signs it | How | Download |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Individual Contributor Assignment Agreement](individual-agreement.md) | Every person who contributes | Online, through CLA Assistant on your first pull request | [PDF](individual-agreement.pdf) |
| [Entity Contributor Assignment Agreement](entity-agreement.md) | A company whose employees contribute, if it owns their work | Signed by someone authorised to sign for the company, and sent to chronitonlabs-legal@axonops.com | [PDF](entity-agreement.pdf) |

The current version is 1.0, published 27 October 2026. Earlier versions, once there are any, are kept under [Releases](https://github.com/chronitonlabs/contributor-agreements/releases).

If your employer signs the Entity agreement, you still sign the Individual agreement yourself.

## How signing works

We use [CLA Assistant](https://cla-assistant.io/) to collect signatures for the Individual agreement. It is a free, open source service that SAP has run since 2014, and it is widely used by open source projects, including Grafana, Mapbox and smallstep.

1. When you open your first pull request on a Chroniton Labs project, CLA Assistant adds a comment and a check to it, with a link to sign.
2. The link asks you to sign in with your GitHub account. This only identifies which account is signing.
3. You read the agreement, enter your full legal name, and, only if you are also signing for your employer or another rights owner, its full legal name.
4. You click to accept. Your typed name and acceptance are your signature.
5. The check on your pull request turns green. You do not need to sign again for later pull requests or other Chroniton Labs projects.

CLA Assistant records your name, GitHub username, the date and the version you signed. If we publish a new version of the agreement, CLA Assistant asks you to sign it on your next pull request. SAP's [privacy statement for CLA Assistant](https://gist.github.com/CLAassistant/3a73e4cd729c9d0a6e30#file-privacy-md) covers what it keeps.

## The agreements in plain English

Contributor agreements can make people nervous, and with good reason: some companies have used them to take community code and change its licence later. This section explains, in plain words, why that cannot happen here and why nothing in our agreements is new.

This summary is not part of the agreements. If anything here differs from them, the agreements apply.

**Our promise: Apache License 2.0, always.** AxonOps promises, in the agreement itself, that your contributions will only ever be licensed under the Apache License, Version 2.0. No licence change, no proprietary version, no bait and switch. The promise is legally binding and you can enforce it yourself. AxonOps cannot get out of it by selling the code either: it can only transfer the rights to someone who agrees in writing to keep the same promise.

**Nothing new.** We did not write our own agreements from scratch. They are built from the [Project Harmony](https://www.harmonyagreements.org/) templates, a standard set of contributor agreements drafted with lawyers and open source communities. Canonical uses Harmony agreements for its open source projects, including LXD, MicroK8s, Snapcraft, Juju and Multipass. Assigning copyright with a binding promise to keep the code open is also long established: the Free Software Foundation has asked contributors to GNU projects to do the same for decades. The agreements are governed by the law of England and Wales.

**Who you are signing with.** AxonOps Limited, a UK company, is the steward of the Chroniton Labs projects. Chroniton Labs is the name the projects are published under, not a separate company.

**What you give.** You assign the copyright in your contributions to AxonOps, and give AxonOps a licence to any of your patents that your contribution needs. One owner means AxonOps can stand behind the code, defend it if someone disputes who owns it, and give it back to Apache projects without tracking down every contributor again.

**What you keep.** AxonOps immediately gives you back a licence to do anything you like with your own contribution, for any purpose, under any terms you choose. You can reuse it in your own projects, at work or anywhere else.

**Giving back to Apache.** Many Chroniton Labs projects build on projects of The Apache Software Foundation, such as ChronitonDB on Apache Cassandra. We owe those projects a lot, and we offer our fixes and features back to them. So we added the one thing most contributor agreements do not need: a narrow exception to the Apache License promise that lets AxonOps give your contribution to The Apache Software Foundation, under its own contributor agreements, for inclusion in one of its projects. It cannot be used for anything else.

**AI-assisted contributions are welcome.** You can use AI tools for any part of your contribution. You review what you submit and take responsibility for it, as with any other tool. You only promise about the rights you actually have, not that every line is copyrightable.

**If your employer owns your work.** Many employment contracts give the employer the copyright in what you write. If that applies, your employer either signs the Entity agreement, or authorises you to sign on its behalf, or has given you the rights. You tell us which. We rely on what you tell us and do not check your contract.

**Code you did not write.** If your contribution includes code from somewhere else, such as another open source project, you say so, say where it came from and keep its licence notices. The agreement does not transfer anyone else's rights.

**New versions.** We may publish new versions of the agreements. A new version only applies to contributions you make after you choose to sign it.

**No obligations on you.** You do not have to keep contributing, support your code, or give any warranty about it beyond what the agreement says.

## Your information

When you sign, we keep your full legal name, your GitHub username, the date and the version you signed. The [AxonOps privacy policy](https://axonops.com/privacy/) explains what we keep, why, and your rights. Questions go to chronitonlabs-legal@axonops.com.

## Signing as a company

Complete the [Entity agreement](entity-agreement.md), including Schedule A listing the people who contribute for you. Have it signed by someone authorised to sign for your company, and email it to chronitonlabs-legal@axonops.com or post it to AxonOps Limited, 124 City Road, London, EC1V 2NX, England. Before you send it, tell the people listed in Schedule A, as the agreement explains.
